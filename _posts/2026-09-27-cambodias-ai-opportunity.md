Artificial intelligence is moving quickly from chatbots to systems that can understand documents, images, maps, speech, and data. New AI models can analyse complex problems, predict future events, and complete carefully defined digital tasks. For Cambodia, the opportunity is not to compete with the world's largest technology companies by building a frontier model from scratch. It is to use existing AI systems to solve national problems more quickly and effectively.

The first priority should be infrastructure. AI can analyse satellite images, drone footage, and road inspections to identify potholes, damaged bridges, blocked drainage systems, flooding, and construction delays. It can also compare a project's physical progress with its contracts, budgets, invoices, and engineering reports. This would help authorities detect problems earlier, direct maintenance funds more efficiently, and improve accountability in major infrastructure projects.

Flood management is another important area. Cambodia could combine rainfall data, river levels, terrain maps, drainage information, and satellite imagery to predict flooding and provide earlier warnings. Similar systems could support traffic management, electricity-demand forecasting, waste collection, water supplies, and the maintenance of public buildings. These applications would be especially valuable as cities expand and climate risks increase.

AI could also improve everyday government services. Khmer-language assistants could help citizens understand regulations, complete forms, check application status, and find information about taxes, permits, education, health, and social services. Document-processing systems could help officials handle land records, customs paperwork, business registrations, and construction permits more quickly.

However, successful AI adoption will depend less on the model itself than on the quality of Cambodia's digital foundations. The country needs secure digital identity, reliable connectivity, interoperable government databases, common data standards, strong cybersecurity, and effective privacy protection. It also needs high-quality Khmer datasets for speech recognition, translation, optical character recognition, and official public-service information.

The government should begin with a small number of practical pilot projects rather than launching many disconnected experiments. Good starting points would include AI-assisted road inspections, flood early-warning systems, infrastructure-project monitoring, procurement analysis, and Khmer-language citizen support. Each project should have a clear owner, a defined budget, baseline performance data, and measurable targets such as reduced costs, faster services, fewer delays, or improved accuracy.

Human oversight must remain central. AI should be allowed to summarise, predict, flag, and recommend, but it should not independently decide land disputes, welfare eligibility, immigration cases, policing actions, medical treatment, or other matters that affect people's rights. Citizens should know when AI is being used, understand how important decisions are made, and have access to human review and appeal.

Cambodia should also develop local capability. Universities can train engineers and researchers in AI, data science, cybersecurity, and public policy. Cambodian companies can build applications for agriculture, logistics, tourism, manufacturing, and government services. International partners can provide funding, technical expertise, cloud access, and training, while Cambodia retains control over its public data and national priorities.

One strong national project would be an AI-powered infrastructure monitoring platform. It could combine contracts, budgets, invoices, satellite images, drone photographs, inspection reports, and citizen complaints. The system would identify possible delays, cost increases, poor-quality work, and maintenance needs, while human officials would verify the findings before taking action.

Cambodia's AI strategy should therefore focus on practical results rather than technology demonstrations. The country does not need to build the biggest model; it needs to build the most useful and trusted systems. With strong digital foundations, Khmer-language capability, skilled institutions, and clear safeguards, AI could help Cambodia deliver infrastructure faster, improve public services, reduce waste, and build more resilient cities [\[un\]](https://www.un.org/ohrlls/sites/www.un.org.ohrlls/files/cambodia_ldc_dpoa_national_report_2026.pdf).
